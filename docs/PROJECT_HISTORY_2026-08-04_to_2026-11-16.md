# Project ATLAS — Engineering history

**Coverage:** repository inception on 4 August 2026 through the latest reviewed engineering evidence dated 16 November 2026.

This is a curated milestone history, not a replacement for the commit graph, raw test logs or dated engineering reports. It distinguishes a commissioned baseline from later changes, and a software test from physical acceptance.

## 4 August 2026 — Day 1: navigation foundation audit

The initial read-only audit found live LiDAR and ultrasonic telemetry, but important navigation foundations were not yet trustworthy:

- Odometry was integrated from commanded velocity rather than calibrated wheel travel.
- Encoder response was asymmetric; only M1 showed a clear responsive channel in the initial lifted-wheel test.
- An active EKF was not found in that source snapshot.
- LiDAR TF definitions and base-frame ownership were inconsistent.
- IMU orientation and mounting needed physical verification.

The audit prescribed an evidence-first correction order: verify each encoder, measure wheel geometry, establish encoder-derived odometry, verify sensor mounting and TF, then introduce/tune EKF and navigation.

Source: [Navigation foundation audit — 4 August](NAVIGATION_FOUNDATION_AUDIT_2026-08-04.md).

## 6–10 August — platform commissioning and autonomy software

The 6 August commissioning report records the Jetson Orin Nano Super migration and an initial baseline for manual drive, four motor/encoder channels, measured short movements, SLAM, Nav2, autonomous exploration, map save and reboot/autostart. It records a physical return-to-home result within approximately 4 cm for that hardware/configuration snapshot.

The 10 August software commissioning report documents a user-managed EKF, deterministic IDLE/MAPPING/LOCALIZATION modes, named places, a bounded experience store and a mission-agent layer that dispatches only allowlisted high-level tools behind deterministic safety checks and operator confirmation.

## 24 August – early September — mapping and recovery reliability

Subsequent records show that early successful commissioning did not guarantee repeatable room-to-room navigation. Tight-space recovery, saved-map identity, localization continuity and sensor/USB recovery were investigated. Work shifted toward preserving recordings, distinguishing map quality from pose quality, and preventing recovery or startup logic from silently creating motion authority.

See the dated [August localization validation](validation/2026-08-24-tight-recovery-localization.md) and the repository [changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

## 10–20 September — encoder, safety and evidence-led commissioning

The 10 September encoder safety validation separated low-speed breakaway/stopping error from encoder telemetry: a short-distance request could overshoot because the PWM floor was too high. The response was to separate manual and autonomous power floors rather than hide the result by changing encoder scale.

On 17 September, M4 feedback was explicitly excluded while a three-encoder degraded configuration was developed. The report records 76 offline regressions and stationary deployment checks, while leaving individual-wheel ground accuracy, arcs and stopping qualification open.

By 20 September, commissioning evidence was being stored with configuration/source hashes and explicit invalidation rules. Historical observations were not silently promoted to physical PASS. Ultrasonic validity and dashboard commissioning views were also expanded, while LiDAR remained the primary navigation geometry source.

## October — localization failure analysis, mapping gates and Astra integration

October investigations found that a successful startup or a stable parked pose could still be globally wrong. Work focused on recorded drives, competing pose hypotheses, AMCL particle support, map/scan fit, encoder-vs-LiDAR motion, startup seeding and stale TF/pose evidence.

The Astra integration went through isolated builds, private ROS domain work, CPU admission checks, depth/IR delivery diagnostics and uncalibrated preview preparation. Several live attempts were correctly stopped by resource guards. A preview or synthetic stream was not treated as calibrated metric 3D mapping.

The repository also gained read-only monitoring and health-reporting work. Monitoring and diagnostics were kept separate from the rover's motion-control path.

Sources: the [main-branch engineering log](../README_ENGINEERING_LOG_LEGACY_2026-10-31.md), [main changelog](../CHANGELOG.md), and the detailed [development-branch blocker ledger](https://github.com/Dhruv0747/ATLAS/blob/claude/jetson-live-snapshot-20261106/docs/audits/ATLAS_CURRENT_BLOCKERS.md).

## 12 November — EKF heading correction and RTAB-Map 3D prototype

The corrected LiDAR-keyframe analysis withdrew an earlier, incorrect IM10A sign diagnosis. The actual issue was that wheel odometry heading/Yahboom gyro orientation disagreed with the independent LiDAR heading, while the EKF fused wheel x/y position. The owner-approved configuration changed the wheel input to velocity-only while retaining IM10A as the yaw-rate source.

Offline replay reduced median heading error from 68° to 1.3°. A controlled live turn showed EKF/IM10A agreement with independent LiDAR to about 3°. However, the same report says full-drive position accuracy remained unverified and the existing map fit was poor in the Hall. A candidate remap was rejected at that stage.

The RTAB-Map 0.23.13 parked prototype used Astra depth, point-cloud conversion and external EKF odometry in an isolated ROS domain. The report records 58 parked snapshots, a live dashboard point display and 35/35 focused tests. This was a mapping pipeline test, not whole-home 3D acceptance.

## 13–14 November — mapping quality, loop closure and map promotion

The development ledger records several remapping attempts, with rejected candidates retained rather than replacing the accepted map. Remap 13 passed its map-fit and closure review, but initially failed the live-heading and start-clearance gates. Following an owner-approved parked review, the Dhruv Room start pose was moved to a location with adequate clearance; map connectivity and both Nav2 path calculations were checked on a copy of the candidate.

Remap 13 was then promoted transactionally as map 38253aab42e9e97304aa, with the previous map retained in accepted backups. The review noted a freshness-evidence deviation: the offline review could not satisfy a single-moment live TF freshness condition, so the evidence sets passed at their respective capture times. This limitation remains visible in the acceptance record.

The same period documented three-encoder qualification work and exclusion of a weak M3 feedback channel. Later physical driving still exposed encoder and turn-sign issues; map promotion did not itself certify autonomous navigation.

Source: [detailed 13–16 November blocker ledger](https://github.com/Dhruv0747/ATLAS/blob/claude/jetson-live-snapshot-20261106/docs/audits/ATLAS_CURRENT_BLOCKERS.md).

## 12–16 November — 3D route mapping and supervised autonomy

The 3D work expanded from parked RTAB-Map tests to recorded room/hall colour and route point clouds. One development record reports 327,802 points in a coloured room/hall map, while a corrected-heading round-trip cloud was separately reported at 47,984 points. These are project-generated artifacts on the rover/development workspace; their existence does not mean the full house has a calibrated, accepted 3D map. Duplicate wall surfaces, lighting, camera/depth extrinsics and incomplete refinement remained known limitations.

On 15 November, the project added map-tap navigation, named waypoints, Manual/Autonomous controls, a three-encoder consensus change and closed-loop speed control. The first autonomous movement exposed an excessive speed caused by an open-loop PWM floor; the manual-only lock was restored while speed calibration was completed. The later speed check passed twice at the requested 0.15 m/s setting, with measured means of 0.116 and 0.096 m/s.

On 16 November, the seventh Stage-3 supervised round-trip attempt produced the first measured safe-stop PASS: 0.14 s and 0.006 m after the operator pressed the physical B stop while Nav2 was moving. The full route still failed: the Hall door's right-side no-echo region blocked the outbound leg, the owner assisted the rover into/out of the Hall, one encoder-critical sample occurred, and turn-sign error was 5.8% against a 5% limit.

The same day, an owner-tested dashboard abort path was verified: autonomy disarmed, Nav2 cancellation acknowledged and the rover-stopped message recorded. CPU load during a parked/charging sample was high, and the owner reported CPU reaching 100% during drives with the dashboard becoming unresponsive. Drive-load budgeting therefore remains an acceptance blocker.

The human-aware navigation document dated 16 November is a design only. Radar tracking, YOLO person detection and Astra depth are not yet a completed, validated person-aware navigation feature.

## 16 November — evening and night deployment (approximately 19:00–22:30)

The night session added important live changes after Stage-3 attempt 7:

- At about 19:30, the Hall named place was moved to (1.68, −5.35), with approximately 1.1 m wall clearance, and Nav2's progress allowance was raised from 20 s to 45 s. The safety guard thresholds were not relaxed.
- Front and rear ultrasonic channels were confirmed VALID at approximately 3.4 Hz with ~0.4 s sample age. Left and right channels were DISABLED. A fresh-valid, reach-based ultrasonic coverage rule was deployed around 19:45 to help the LiDAR availability guard with front/rear blind bins; it cannot clear a bin when a short obstacle range is reported.
- At about 21:00, the Stage-3 #7 result was consolidated: safe stop passed at 0.14 s / 0.006 m, but the Hall round trip failed at the right-side no-echo wall. One CRITICAL encoder sample and a 5.8% turn-sign discrepancy also failed their criteria. The same session recorded high parked/charging CPU use: 371% aggregate across six cores, with owner-reported 100% CPU and dashboard unresponsiveness during drives.
- At about 22:00, the drive CPU policy was changed so the AI annotator no longer starts for autonomy demand and the agent-team service has no demand triggers. The voice companion was deliberately kept enabled because it provides spoken stop/E-STOP commands, but its priority was lowered to Nice 19 / CPUWeight 10 while retaining its 25% CPU quota.
- Mission abort was added to the dashboard: it first disarms autonomy to Manual, then cancels Nav2 and commands stop. The owner verified the dashboard action: autonomy OFF, Nav2 cancellation acknowledged and rover-stopped status. A 30-second abort banner and higher-contrast dashboard feedback were then deployed.
- The 22:30 deployment record says the web, autonomy, narrator, feature manager and voice-companion units were active with the rover parked, E-STOP latched and autonomy OFF. The owner subsequently verified the abort action. A real-drive CPU improvement and successful full round trip were not yet proven.

This night work is drawn from the dated live blocker ledger and must not be confused with the documentation-only changes in this pull request.

## Evidence vocabulary

- **Implemented:** code or configuration exists.
- **Offline-tested:** tested without changing live rover behavior.
- **Deployed:** installed on the Jetson or dashboard.
- **Live-observed:** measured on the running system.
- **Physically accepted:** the relevant real-world acceptance criteria passed.
- **Rejected / blocked:** the candidate or test did not pass its stated gate.

A PASS in one category does not imply PASS in the next.
